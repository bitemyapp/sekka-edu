title: Snowflake Bentley
level: 4
skills: vocabulary, fluency
cover: snowflake, #3a7fb0
about: The true story of a Vermont farm boy who spent his life photographing snowflakes.

Wilson Bentley was born in 1865 on a farm in Jericho, Vermont, where the winters were long and the snow fell deep. Most people saw snow as something to shovel. Willie saw it as a treasure.
===
When he was fifteen, his mother gave him an old microscope. Willie used it to look at everything he could find: drops of water, bits of stone, feathers. But what amazed him most were snowflakes. Under the lens, each one was a tiny six-sided star, more delicate than lace.
===
Willie tried to draw the snowflakes he saw, but they melted before he could finish. He needed a faster way to capture them. So he saved his money until his parents helped him buy a special camera that could be joined to a microscope. It cost about as much as ten cows.
===
At first, every picture he took came out blurry. Willie kept trying. He caught snowflakes on a black tray, picked them up with a thin splinter of wood, and held his breath so he would not melt them. At last, on January 15, 1885, when he was nineteen, he took the first photograph of a single snowflake.
===
Some of his neighbors thought he was wasting his time. But Willie went on photographing snowflakes for the rest of his life, more than five thousand of them, and he never found two that were exactly alike. In 1931, his pictures were published in a book called Snow Crystals, and scientists and artists still study them today.

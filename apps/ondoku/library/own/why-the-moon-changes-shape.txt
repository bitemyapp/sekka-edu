title: Why the Moon Changes Shape
level: 4
skills: vocabulary, fluency
cover: moon, #33407a
about: The moon looks round one week and like a fingernail the next. Here is what is really going on.

Look up at the moon a few nights in a row and you will notice something strange: it seems to change shape. Some nights it is a thin, curved sliver. Some nights it is half a circle. And some nights it is a perfect, glowing ball. But the moon itself never changes shape at all.
===
The moon is a ball of rock, and it does not make any light of its own. It shines because sunlight falls on it and bounces off, the way a ball in a dark room shines when someone points a flashlight at it. At every moment, one half of the moon is lit by the sun, and the other half is dark.
===
The moon travels around the Earth, taking about a month to go all the way around. As it moves, we see the lit half from different angles. When the moon is between the Earth and the sun, its lit side faces away from us, and we can hardly see it at all. This is called a new moon.
===
Night after night, the moon moves farther along its path, and we see more of its sunny side: first a thin crescent, then a half moon, then a bulging shape called a gibbous moon. When the Earth is between the sun and the moon, we see the whole lit side at once. That is a full moon.
===
After the full moon, the lit part we can see grows smaller again, until the moon disappears into another new moon, and the cycle starts over. It takes about twenty-nine and a half days from one new moon to the next. People have used this rhythm to keep track of time for thousands of years. In fact, the word "month" comes from the word "moon."

## Editing notes

- Removed: Gutenberg header, footer and licence; illustration markers and captions; Roman-numeral stanza and lesson numbers; front matter and "THE END".
- Typography: `_italic_` underscores dropped; `--` became `—`; prose rejoined to one paragraph per line; verse keeps its lines. Townsend's and Kipling's small-capital openings ("A HARE", "IN the sea") are set in normal case.
- Titles: each fable and poem in a collection, and each excerpted chapter, starts with its title as its own line. The Mother Goose rhymes and McGuffey lessons have no title lines.
- Year: the first publication of the work, except Aesop, which uses Townsend's translation (1867). The Grimm year is the German original (1812); the English text is the Taylor and Edwardes translation.
- Three transcription slips fixed: in The Velveteen Rabbit, "HERE was once" (the decorative drop-cap "T" was lost) became "There was once", and "germs!-Burn" became "germs!—Burn". In McGuffey lesson LI, "dinner with us. Fido?" became "dinner with us, Fido?".

## Left out

- **Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star**: not in *The Real Mother Goose* (#10607). It is Jane Taylor's poem and would need another source.
- **The Tale of Benjamin Bunny** (#14407): the brief asked for Benjamin Bunny or Squirrel Nutkin, and Nutkin was chosen. In Benjamin Bunny, old Mr. Bunny smokes a pipe, kicks the cat into the greenhouse, and whips Benjamin and Peter with a switch.
- **Henny-Penny** (*English Fairy Tales*, #7439): Foxy-woxy snaps off the heads of Turkey-lurkey, Goosey-poosey and Ducky-daddles one after another and throws their bodies over his shoulder. That is more graphic than the rest of the shelf.
- **The Story of the Three Bears** (*English Fairy Tales*, #7439): this is Southey's version with an old woman, not Goldilocks. The narrator calls her "impudent, bad" and her head "ugly, dirty", and wonders whether she "broke her neck" or was sent to the House of Correction "for a vagrant". That is a demeaning picture of a poor old woman.
- **How the Leopard Got His Spots** (*Just So Stories*, #2781): contains a racial slur and demeaning depictions of an Ethiopian man.
- **Other McGuffey lessons**: I–X are word drills too short to read as stories. Also left out: lessons about whips (XI, XLI, XLVII), God and prayer (LVI, LVIII), obedience and school sermons (XXXIX, LXIII), the orphaned lame boy (LIX–LX), a rat caught by the cat and suffering (LII), and a lost boy (XLIV).
